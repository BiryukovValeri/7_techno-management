## 2. Definition
Архитектура Переговоров is a technology for managing the company's negotiation power in external and internal decisions.

Negotiation power in this corpus is not domination over another party. It is the ability to enter a negotiation/decision with explicit goals, alternatives, boundaries, influence map, managed concessions and a defined next step.

Status: SOURCE CONFIRMED — Technology 04 + Glossary + current product protocols.

## 3. Object of management
The object is the architecture of a concrete negotiation or internal decision:
- situation and price of error;
- goals, interests and success criteria;
- decision participant/counterparty profile;
- influence, power, roles and authority;
- BATNA / best alternative;
- ZOPA / zone of possible agreement;
- concession boundaries and countersteps;
- cognitive risks;
- next-decision scenario;
- result fixation, review and next cycle.

The technology does not manage the other party's will and does not guarantee agreement.

Status: SOURCE CONFIRMED.

## 4. Application contours
One core is used in two application fields.

External contour: clients, suppliers, partners, investors, contractors, retail chains and other external counterparties.

Internal contour: owner, management, finance, sales, procurement, production/operations and project teams.

A recurring corpus principle is that an external position can be weakened before the meeting when internal goals, authority, red lines, concession price, risks and fallback scenario are not aligned.

Status: SOURCE CONFIRMED.

## 5. Target organizational context
Current operating protocols explicitly address medium business and work with the owner plus management team. Functional roles vary by situation and are not a fixed organization model.

## 6. Problem class
The technology applies to real external or internal negotiations/decisions where a bad decision can create material loss of money, margin, time, control, relationships, obligations, resources or responsibility.

Source-confirmed failure patterns include:
- entering without explicit goal/minimum;
- weak or absent BATNA;
- no boundary of a bad agreement;
- treating the formal negotiator as the whole decision system;
- concessions without explicit cost/counterstep;
- internal disagreement over authority, red lines or risk;
- cognitive pressure/anchoring/loss aversion/group dynamics/inertia affecting a concrete decision;
- oral result without facts, owners, deadlines and learning.

No universal quantified loss claim is inferred.

## 7. Canonical mechanism
Current operating chain:
**situation -> price of error -> goal/minimum -> side profile -> influence map -> BATNA -> ZOPA -> concessions/countersteps -> cognitive risks -> scenario -> fixation -> review -> next cycle**

Official 10-step cycle:
1. Situation and price of error.
2. Goals, interests and success criteria.
3. Profile of counterparty / decision participant.
4. Influence and power map.
5. BATNA.
6. ZOPA.
7. Concession boundaries and team roles.
8. Cognitive risks.
9. Next-decision scenario.
10. Result fixation, review and improvement/next cycle.

These are two representations of the same mechanism, not contradictory technologies.

A correct result is not synonymous with “deal closed”. Legitimate outcomes include:
- continue;
- continue under conditions;
- collect/clarify data;
- align internally;
- change the package/position;
- strengthen BATNA;
- pause;
- refuse/exit;
- define conditions for return.

## 15. Known implementation risks
Recorded risks include:
- preparation checklist turning into a full audit;
- mixing BATNA and ZOPA;
- guessing motives in participant profiles;
- role-map bloat;
- insufficient concession-cost calculation;
- psychologizing cognitive risks.

Source-supported controls include separating fact/assumption/hypothesis, keeping BATNA and ZOPA distinct, retaining only decision-relevant roles, and tying cognitive risks to concrete decisions/checks.

Status: SOURCE CONFIRMED.
